# Visual brief: the pilot story

Date: 2027-04-19 (Week 11 lab, Maya Patel, with Leila Ahmed)
From: the master story of 2027-03-22 (`stories/master-story.md`)
Places: Leila Ahmed's list of where the pictures will be used, 2027-04-16
Look: the design guide of 2027-03-01 (`brand/DESIGN.md`)
The page "Uncertain Org | W11 | Visual brief" is in Maya Patel's Claude account.

No picture has been made. This note is the brief for a designer or an image tool.

Rules for every picture: a picture is never proof that something happened. None looks like a photograph of a real event. No number, date, name or claim is drawn inside a picture; figures and their scope go in the caption. An illustration carries the label "Illustration", a diagram "Diagram", a real screen "Real screen". Every picture has a description in words. No logo: the pilot team has none.

## Picture 1: the part that mattered
- Job: picture the part that mattered most to the reader: pick one task you are allowed to use, then check where the answer came from.
- Kind: real screen.
- Place: the pilot update newsletter, in a narrow column.
- Brief: a capture of the "What you can try" part of the newsletter draft, as the draft stands on the day it is taken. Nothing restyled, tidied or edited. Cropped to that part only, tall and narrow. The rest of the page is left out, including the subject lines. Before use, check that only names and roles already in the draft appear (Maya Patel, Ruth Evans) and nothing else private.
- Caption: The "What you can try" part of the newsletter draft, captured on [date]. A draft: nothing has been sent.
- Label: Real screen.
- Description in words: A capture of part of a newsletter draft, under the heading "What you can try". It says to try one task using only material the pilot allows, to check where the answer came from, and to ask Maya Patel if unsure. It is a draft and has not been sent.
- Must not suggest: that the newsletter has been sent or approved; that the method is proven; that the rules apply to other teams.
- Unknown: the capture date, and who takes the capture.

## Picture 2: the need
- Job: set the scene for the need: answers in different places, and no clear sense of which to trust.
- Kind: illustration.
- Place: the top of the article page, wide.
- Brief: a flat, calm scene of a few colleagues standing at a table, looking together at several separate sheets laid out in different places. The sheets do not match each other. Simple figures with no faces and no features that could be a real person. Nothing readable on any sheet. Off-white ground, ink outlines, highlights as filled shapes only. Wide, about three times as wide as tall. Leave out screens with writing, logos, any text, and any count of people that could read as twelve or nine.
- Caption: An illustration of colleagues looking at several separate documents together.
- Label: Illustration.
- Description in words: An illustration of a few simple figures without faces, standing around a table covered with separate sheets of paper and looking at them together. Nothing on the sheets can be read.
- Must not suggest: a real event or real people; a workshop; a head count; that the problem is solved; a result.

## Picture 3: invited, came, used a skill again
- Job: show the three stages: people invited, people who came, and people who said they used a skill again. Asked for by Sam Brooks, team analyst.
- Kind: diagram.
- Place: slide 3 of the managers' deck.
- Brief: three plain shapes in a row, left to right, joined by arrows. All three the same size and the same fill, so nothing reads as a measured drop. Shapes only: no words, numbers or names inside. Ink outline on the off-white ground, in the shape of the slide.
- Caption: 12 invited, 9 came to the first workshop on 8 February 2027, and 6 of those 9 told Maya Patel they used a skill again the following week. That is what they said: nobody was timed or checked. These twelve people only.
- Label: Diagram.
- Description in words: A diagram of three equal shapes in a row, joined by arrows. The three stages are invited, came, and used a skill again. The amounts are in the caption.
- Must not suggest: that the nine are known by name; that the six is a measured result; that it covers anyone beyond these twelve.

## Picture 4: one more small trial
- Job: show one more small step, with the open questions staying open.
- Kind: diagram.
- Place: slide 4 of the managers' deck, the request.
- Brief: one solid shape on the left for the trial. To its right, a few empty shapes with dashed outlines for what is still open, spaced apart, with no arrows into them and nothing at the far end that looks like a finish line. The number of empty shapes carries no meaning. Shapes only, no text. Ink on the off-white ground, in the shape of the slide.
- Caption: One more small trial: people outside the pilot try one task they are allowed to use and check where the answer came from. This is a plan, not a result. Its size, dates and people are not decided, and whether to roll out is still open.
- Label: Diagram.
- Description in words: A diagram of one solid shape on the left and a few empty dashed shapes to its right, with no arrows between them. The solid shape stands for the one small trial. The dashed shapes stand for what is still open.
- Must not suggest: roll-out; approval; a result; a finish line.

## Picture 5: a chapter opener, one pattern
- Job: one calm picture to open each chapter of the interactive story, all from the same pattern.
- Kind: illustration.
- Place: the interactive story, one picture a chapter, read mostly on phones.
- Brief: a small set of simple shapes, rearranged calmly for each chapter, in the off-white, ink and one highlight fill. A wide strip, about twice as wide as tall, filling the width of a phone. Leave out people, text, ticks, rising bars and upward arrows.
- Caption: each chapter's own caption is written in Week 12. Until then: "An illustration for this chapter."
- Label: Illustration.
- Description in words: An illustration of a few simple shapes arranged calmly to open the chapter. It sets a mood and shows nothing that happened. Each chapter gets its own wording in Week 12.
- Must not suggest: a real scene; progress towards proof; success; that anything has worked.
- Unknown: the chapters, and how many there will be, until the Week 12 lab.
